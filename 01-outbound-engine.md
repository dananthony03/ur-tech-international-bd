# 01. Outbound engine

## Where it started

Day one was 10 Aug 2026. The first weeks ran on Gmail alone, with cold emails written and sent by hand. That gave me a feel for which openings and which roles were worth the effort before any tooling was in place.

## What it became

Period: 10 Aug to 28 Sep 2026.

## How a sequence is built

- One role, one market, one service line per sequence. An operations lead in the UK and a marketing head in Australia never get the same copy.
- Four steps, each under 110 words.
- The first line is a real, dated fact about the prospect's company: a hire, a launch, a growth number, a new market.
- One clear question at the end, and an easy way to say no.

## The sequence families

- Trigger-led batches by market: UK, ANZ and GCC
- Service-led sequences in ANZ: web and growth, software, managed operations and BPO, cloud and IT
- Function-led sequences: operations and technology, marketing and ecommerce
- Vertical sequences: clinics, brokerages, D2C ecommerce
- Theme sequences in the UK: workflow automation, growth, product engineering
- Systems integration across the UK, GCC and Singapore
- Single-account sequences, where one company got its own research and its own copy

## Batches of 25

Prospects are enrolled 25 at a time. A batch is researched, enriched, fit-scored and checked before it goes in, so every send has a reason behind it.

## Two tracks

**Track A** is the sequenced batches with role-matched copy.

**Track B** is for buyers where the research turned up something specific and recent. Those come out of the sequence and get a personal, hand-written email instead.

## Daily routine

Every session starts with a health check across all sequences: delivered, replies and bounces. Then the active batch work, then the next 25. Sends run Monday to Friday in the prospect's local morning.

## Deliverability

A 0.8% bounce rate across 1,311 sends kept the sending domain in good health. The rules that got it there are in [05-tooling-playbook.md](05-tooling-playbook.md).
