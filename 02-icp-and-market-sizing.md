# 02. Customer profile and market sizing

## Week-one research workbook

Before anything was sent, I built a workbook of 200 accounts with 5 personas each, 1,000 targets in all, across the UK, UAE, Germany and France.

## Two-tier customer profile

**Tier A: mid-market, 51 to 500 staff.** Approached on a trigger, such as a senior hire, a funding round, a new market or visible growth. Found through Apollo, ZoomInfo and Lusha.

**Tier B: small businesses, 1 to 50 staff, going digital.** A first or rebuilt website, a first online store, a booking system, a small app. The buyer is the owner, founder or general manager. These are found through Google Places, company registries and social profiles, because the big databases cover small firms poorly.

The two tiers get different sourcing, different copy and a different pace. Tier A is slower and research-heavy. Tier B is volume with fast cycles.

## Filters applied before any list is built

- Company size inside the tier
- A service line the delivery team can take on from India
- Work that needs defence or security clearance is screened out up front, so no time goes into accounts that cannot be served

## How each market was sized

Every geography and segment was sized three ways before it was prioritised.

1. **Top-down.** Start from published industry figures and narrow by geography, sector and company size.
2. **Bottom-up.** Start from one customer and one deal, and scale by what the team can realistically reach and win.
3. **Cross-check.** Compare the two. Where they disagree, go back to the assumptions.

The assumptions are written down each time: reach for the total market, fit for the serviceable part, and win rate for what can be captured in the near term. That way a market choice can be argued on its logic.

## Service lines the profile is built around

- AI-enabled custom development and ERP, new build or upgrade
- KPO/BPO managed services
- Kuzhu HRMS, the company's own HR product
- Web, app and digital marketing, bundled with the lines above
